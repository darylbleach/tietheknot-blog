# Free Wedding Websites UK: What You Get (and What You Don't)

When you're planning a wedding, "free" sounds wonderful. Between the venue, catering, flowers, photographer, and all the rest of it, saving money wherever possible makes obvious sense. So when you search for a wedding website and discover that several platforms offer one at no cost, it's tempting to sign up straight away.

But before you do, it's worth understanding what "free" actually means in this context — because it rarely means the same thing as it does on a price tag.

## What Free Wedding Website Platforms Actually Offer

There are a handful of UK-friendly platforms that let you create a wedding website without paying anything upfront. The most well-known are **Bridebook** and **Hitched**, both of which have a strong UK presence and offer free website tools alongside their broader wedding planning features.

On the plus side, both platforms let you get something online quickly. You can set up a basic page with your wedding date, venue details, and a short message to guests. That's genuinely useful if you're in a hurry and just need somewhere to point people.

Some free platforms also offer basic RSVP collection, though the level of detail you can gather — dietary requirements, song requests, plus-ones — varies considerably and is often limited on free tiers.

## What You Don't Get

Here's where things get more nuanced. If you're weighing up whether free is enough, it helps to know exactly what you're giving up.

### Adverts on Your Wedding Page

Free platforms need to make money somehow, and the most common way is advertising. That means your carefully crafted wedding page may sit alongside adverts for other venues, florists, photographers, or wedding services — brands you've never chosen and may not want associated with your day. You have no control over what appears, and your guests will see it too.

### Limited Design and Customisation

Free wedding websites tend to offer a small number of templates, and meaningful customisation is almost always locked behind a paid upgrade. If you want your site to reflect your colour scheme, your stationery aesthetic, or your general style, a free plan often leaves you frustrated. You'll likely end up with something that looks like hundreds of other couples' websites.

### No Custom Domain

Most free plans give you a URL along the lines of `bridebook.com/your-names-wedding` or similar. It works, but it's not particularly shareable or memorable — and it doesn't feel like yours. Custom domains (something like `daryl-and-sophie.co.uk`) are almost always a paid feature.

### Data and Privacy

When you use a free platform, your guest data — RSVPs, email addresses, dietary needs, phone numbers — sits within that company's systems. Before sharing your guests' personal information with a third-party platform you're not paying for, it's worth reading the privacy policy carefully. Free services have different incentives than paid ones when it comes to data.

## When Free Is Perfectly Fine

Free wedding websites aren't without merit. If your needs are simple — a basic information page, a rough headcount form, and nothing more — a free platform may genuinely do the job. Small, informal celebrations with a limited guest list and no complex logistics don't necessarily need a fully featured website.

Similarly, if you're very early in your planning and just want a temporary placeholder while you sort out the details, a free site can be a sensible starting point. You can always migrate to something more polished once the big decisions are made.

## When It's Worth Paying

For most couples, though, a wedding website ends up doing a lot of heavy lifting. It becomes the central hub for everything: travel directions, hotel recommendations, the gift list, the full schedule of events, dietary requirement collection, and RSVP tracking for dozens — or hundreds — of guests. The more you need your site to do, the more you'll bump into the limits of a free plan.

There's also the question of longevity. Free platforms change their terms, introduce new restrictions, or shut down entirely — sometimes without much warning. If you're planning 12 to 18 months ahead, stability matters.

It's also worth considering the overall cost of your wedding. As we explored in our post on [how much a wedding website costs in the UK](/blog/how-much-does-a-wedding-website-cost-uk), the difference between free and paid is often surprisingly small relative to total wedding spend — and the difference in experience can be significant.
