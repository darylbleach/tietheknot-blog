# Wedding Website RSVP: How to Collect RSVPs Online (UK Guide)

There is a particular kind of dread familiar to most couples a few weeks after sending invitations: the silence. Dozens of envelopes sent, a deadline set, and yet somehow only half your guests have responded — and three of them have replied on a torn-off piece of paper with no name and just the word "yes." If you have experienced this, you are not alone. Traditional RSVP cards are notoriously unreliable, easy to lose, and almost impossible to organise efficiently.

Collecting RSVPs through your wedding website is one of the most practical improvements couples have made to the invitation process in years. It is faster, easier to track, and tends to generate a far higher response rate — particularly among younger guests who would rather click a button than find a stamp.

## Why Online RSVPs Outperform Reply Cards

The case for online RSVPs is straightforward once you have seen the alternative up close.

Reply cards go missing in the post. They arrive without names on them. Guests forget to send them. You end up with a spreadsheet of part-guesses, a stack of envelopes, and an afternoon of cross-referencing. It is not a pleasant way to spend your engagement period.

Online RSVP forms, by contrast, capture the information you need in exactly the format you need it. Every response is time-stamped, attributed to a named guest, and stored in one place. You can check your numbers at any point without sifting through paper. When the caterer asks for final dietary requirements three weeks before the wedding, you have that data ready to export.

There is also an accessibility argument. Many guests — especially older relatives who may struggle with handwriting or poor eyesight — find a simple web form considerably easier to complete than filling in a small card.

## What to Include on Your RSVP Form

The exact questions you ask will depend on your wedding, but most couples find it useful to collect the following:

**Attendance confirmation** is the obvious starting point — a simple yes or no to the invitation. If you have different groups attending different parts of the day (ceremony only versus full reception, for example), your form should make that distinction clear.

**Full name** seems obvious, but it is worth asking for explicitly. On reply cards, guests often omit it entirely. On a web form, you can make it a required field.

**Dietary requirements and allergies** are essential for catering. A free-text box works for most couples, though some prefer dropdown options for common requirements (vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, halal, kosher). Whatever you choose, make sure your caterer receives this information in a format they can work with.

**Plus-one details** need careful handling. If you have given some guests a plus-one, your form should let them add a partner's name and dietary requirements. If you have not offered a plus-one to everyone, your form should either not show the option for those guests, or make clear that it applies only where specified on the invitation.

**Song requests** are optional, but they make the form feel a little more celebratory. Asking guests what they would like to hear on the dance floor is a nice touch — and genuinely useful for briefing your DJ or band.

## Setting Your RSVP Deadline

Give yourself enough time to chase non-responders and pass final numbers to your venue and caterer. Most UK venues ask for a final headcount somewhere between two and four weeks before the wedding date.

Working back from that, a sensible rule of thumb is to set your RSVP deadline six to eight weeks before the wedding. That gives you a week or two of buffer to chase stragglers before you need to confirm numbers. If you are getting married during a busy period — summer weekends, bank holidays — guests may have competing commitments and appreciate a slightly longer lead time.

Display your deadline prominently on your wedding website, not just buried in the RSVP form. If guests have to hunt for the date, some will not bother.

## Chasing Non-Responders: A Practical Approach

Accept that you will need to chase roughly 20 to 30 percent of your guests, even with an online form. People get busy, forget, or assume someone else has responded on their behalf.

A week before your deadline, send a polite reminder to those who have not yet replied. If your wedding website lets you see who has and has not responded, this is easy to do precisely. Without that visibility, you are essentially guessing.

Keep the message light. Something along the lines of "we just wanted to make sure you received our RSVP link — the deadline is the [date] and we would love to know if you are able to join us" is sufficient. There is no need for urgency or guilt.
